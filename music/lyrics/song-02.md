# Mine Own and Not Mine Own

[Verse 1]
You loved him first, Helena,
before he turned his oaths toward Hermia.
Now Theseus counts four days to wed Hippolyta,
and Egeus tells the court his daughter must obey:
marry Demetrius, or the law decides,
death, or a nun's life under a barren moon.
She loves Lysander. Tomorrow night
they'll slip through the wood to his aunt's, past the rule.

[Verse 2]
They trust you with the secret as they go.
You carry it to Demetrius, hoping he'll be grateful;
he runs into the wood for Hermia, and you run after him.
That same night Quince's workmen gather in the trees
to rehearse a play for the wedding:
Bottom the weaver as Pyramus,
kept from his Thisbe by a wall.

[Chorus]
Mine own, and not mine own,
a jewel I found but never earned.
Mine own, and not mine own,
who lit the lamp in him when he turned?

[Verse 3]
In that wood a fairy king and queen are at war
over a boy whose mother, Titania's friend, has died;
she keeps him for her sake. Their quarrel floods the fields
and puts frost on summer roses. Oberon wants the boy.
He sends Puck for a flower: a drop on sleeping eyes,
and the waker loves the first live thing it sees.

[Verse 4]
He watches Demetrius threaten you and go,
and softens: Puck, charm that Athenian for her.
He paints his sleeping queen. Puck finds Athenian clothes:
Lysander, not Demetrius. The wrong eyes.
You wake him; he swears he loves you, leaves her sleeping.
You think it's cruel mockery, and run.
Hermia wakes from a serpent dream, alone.

[Chorus]
Mine own, and not mine own,
a jewel I found but never earned.
Mine own, and not mine own,
who lit the lamp in him when he turned?

[Verse 5]
Puck gives Bottom a donkey's head; his friends flee.
Titania wakes, enchanted, and adores him; fairies wait on him.
Oberon sees the mistake and touches Demetrius' eyes himself.
Now both men swear love to you. You're sure it's a game,
and Hermia's in on it; she thinks you stole her love.
The men walk off to duel.

[Bridge]
Puck pulls down the fog and borrows their voices,
leads them apart till all four fall asleep.
He cures Lysander's eyes. Only his.
Demetrius still wears the flower.

[Verse 6]
Oberon says the charmed queen gave him the boy.
He frees her eyes; Puck lifts the donkey head from Bottom's own,
and the fairy rulers make their peace.
Horns: Theseus finds you sleeping and overrules Egeus;
three couples will marry, his among them.
Lysander loves Hermia again. Demetrius, still enchanted,
says some power brought him back to you, Helena.
Bottom wakes, can't tell his dream, and finds his friends.

[Verse 7]
Married now, you watch the workmen's play:
lovers whisper through a Wall; a Lion stains Thisbe's dropped cloak;
Pyramus thinks she's dead and stabs himself;
she finds him and does the same. Only acting.
Bottom sits up; the court laughs; the players dance.

[Final Chorus]
Mine own, and not mine own,
a jewel I found but never earned.
Mine own, and not mine own,
who lit the lamp in him when he turned?

[Outro]
Fairies bless the beds while the house sleeps.
Puck asks: if we offended, think it a dream,
and give us your hands.
Mine own, and not mine own.
