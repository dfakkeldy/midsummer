# Before the First Cock Crow

[Verse 1]
Daylight in Athens. Theseus counts four days
till he weds Hippolyta. Then Egeus makes his case:
Hermia must wed Demetrius,
or die, or live her whole life as a nun.
She loves Lysander. Whispering, they choose:
meet in the wood tomorrow night,
then on to his aunt's, beyond the law.

[Verse 2]
Helena loves Demetrius, who left her for Hermia.
She tells him the plan, hoping he'll be grateful.
Nightfall: he hunts the lovers; she hunts him.
Out the same gate, Quince leads his craftsmen
to rehearse a play for the wedding feast,
Pyramus and Thisbe, Bottom the weaver as Pyramus.

[Chorus]
Before the first cock crow, before the lark,
set it right, set it right in the dark.
Every heart's a wager, every eye's a mark,
before the first cock crow, before the lark.

[Verse 3]
Midnight. The fairy king and queen collide
over a boy she raises for her dead friend's sake.
Their quarrel swells the rivers, scrambles the seasons.
Oberon wants the boy. He sends Puck for a flower:
one drop on sleeping lids, and the sleeper wakes
in love with the first live creature in sight.
He hears Demetrius spurn Helena: charm that man.

[Verse 4]
Later. Oberon streaks his sleeping queen.
Puck finds a man asleep in Athenian clothes,
Lysander, not Demetrius, and charms the wrong eyes.
Helena shakes him; he wakes, swears he loves her,
leaves Hermia lying on the ground.
Helena thinks he mocks her, and runs;
Hermia wakes, calls his name, hears nothing.

[Chorus]
Before the first cock crow, before the lark,
set it right, set it right in the dark.
Every heart's a wager, every eye's a mark,
before the first cock crow, before the lark.

[Verse 5]
Small hours. Puck crowns Bottom with a donkey's head;
the craftsmen flee. Titania wakes, enchanted,
loves him, and sets her fairies to serve him.
Oberon finds the error and charms Demetrius himself.
Two men court Helena now; she calls it mockery.
Hermia feels betrayed. The rivals head off to duel.

[Bridge]
Puck drops the fog, speaks in each man's voice,
leads them apart until they fall.
Four asleep. He cures Lysander's eyes, only his;
Demetrius still wears the flower.

[Verse 6]
Grey light. Oberon says the charmed queen gave up the boy.
He frees her; Puck lifts the donkey head from Bottom's own.
King and queen dance, at peace. Then dawn horns:
Theseus finds the four and overrules Egeus;
three couples will wed, his own among them.
Lysander, cured, is Hermia's again;
Demetrius, still enchanted, loves Helena.
Bottom wakes past words and runs back to his crew.

[Verse 7]
Wedding night. Three couples watch the play:
a Wall, a Moon; the Lion stains Thisbe's cloak;
Pyramus thinks she's dead, stabs himself;
she finds him, does the same. Just actors.
The court jokes, Bottom springs up for a dance,
and midnight sends the lovers to their beds.

[Final Chorus]
Before the first cock crow, before the lark,
set it right, set it right in the dark.
Every heart's a wager, every eye's a mark,
before the first cock crow, before the lark.

[Outro]
Fairies bless the house before the day.
Puck says, if we offended, think you slept;
pardon us, and give us your hands.
Before the lark.
