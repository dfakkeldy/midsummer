# Lord, What Fools

[Verse 1]
Listen. Four days till Theseus weds Hippolyta,
then Egeus storms the court with his daughter, Hermia:
she weds Demetrius, his pick, or she meets the law,
death, or a nun's cell, cold moon and all.
She loves Lysander, so the pair make a plan:
tomorrow night, through my wood, hand in hand,
to his aunt's house, out where Athens can't reach.

[Verse 2]
Helena loves Demetrius; he swore to her once,
then switched to Hermia. So she spills what she knows,
hoping for thanks. He chases them; she chases him.
Meanwhile Quince rounds up his working men,
Bottom the weaver cast as Pyramus,
rehearsing Pyramus and Thisbe for the wedding,
in my wood by moonlight. Bless 'em.

[Chorus]
Lord, what fools these mortals be,
running through the moonlight after what they can't see.
Lord, what fools, what fools these mortals be,
and the biggest fool's the one who swears he's free.

[Verse 3]
My king and queen? At war. She keeps a boy,
her dead friend's son, and she won't hand him over.
Their fight floods fields, puts frost on the roses.
So he sends me flying for a flower:
juice on sleeping eyes, you'll dote
on the first live thing you notice.
He hears Demetrius tell Helena to get lost:
Puck, charm that Athenian. Done. I'm gone.

[Verse 4]
He streaks the queen's eyes while she sleeps in her bower.
Me? I find Athenian clothes on a sleeping man,
a girl lying further off. Job done. Wrong man.
That was Lysander. Helena shakes him awake;
he swears he loves her, leaves Hermia cold.
Helena thinks he's mocking her and runs;
Hermia wakes alone, screaming his name.

[Chorus]
Lord, what fools these mortals be,
running through the moonlight after what they can't see.
Lord, what fools, what fools these mortals be,
and the biggest fool's the one who swears he's free.

[Verse 5]
Then I catch the players rehearsing near her bed,
give Bottom a donkey's head, just the head,
and his crew flies like geese at a gunshot.
Titania wakes, charmed, loves him, has her fairies wait on him.
My king sees my slip and charms Demetrius himself.
Now two men chase Helena; she says it's a cruel joke.
Hermia thinks she's been robbed. The boys go off to duel.

[Bridge]
So I roll in the fog, throw each rival's voice,
lead 'em up and down till they drop.
Four on the ground. Cure on Lysander's eyes,
only his. Demetrius stays charmed. Not my call.

[Verse 6]
My king says the queen, still charmed, gave up the boy.
He frees her eyes; I pull the donkey head off,
Bottom's own face safe underneath. King and queen dance.
Horns. Theseus finds the four, overrules Egeus.
Lysander's cured and back with Hermia;
Demetrius, still charmed, loves Helena;
three couples wed, the duke's among them.
Bottom wakes, can't explain his dream, runs to his crew.

[Verse 7]
Feast night, three couples watch the play:
a Wall with a chink, a Lion stains Thisbe's cloak,
Pyramus thinks she's dead and stabs himself;
she finds him, does the same. Fake blades.
Bottom pops back up; the court cracks jokes; two players dance.

[Final Chorus]
Lord, what fools these mortals be,
running through the moonlight after what they can't see.
Lord, what fools, what fools these mortals be,
and the biggest fool's the one who swears he's free.

[Outro]
Then we fairies bless the house and every bed.
If we shadows have offended, call it a dream;
pardon us, and give me your hands,
and Robin makes amends. Lord, what fools.
